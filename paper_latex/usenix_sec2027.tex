\title{\Large \bf A Release Test Is Not Runtime Authority:\\

\author{}

\begin{abstract}
Finite release tests and handoff mechanisms support different safety claims.
We study two transitions in learning-enabled control: from shielded adaptation
to raw-policy execution, and from a monitored policy to a fallback controller.
In a controlled Safe-Control-Gym audit, 20 independently trained controllers
yielded 480 snapshot--state pairs accepted by a five-step raw-policy test.  When
this bounded evidence was extrapolated to 120-step deployment, raw execution
violated constraints on 10 pairs across five training runs.  A repeated
raw-policy lookahead switcher avoided these failures but did not establish
fallback recoverability.  Under bounded reward-record poisoning that remained
within known-sign and calibrated scalar-envelope predicates, raw execution
failed on 46/116 accepted pairs and the lookahead switcher on 34/116.  All
switcher failures followed transfer to an integrity-protected linear-quadratic
regulator (LQR) fallback without certified handoff recovery; seven occurred
only under the switcher.  Evidence about the outgoing policy therefore did not
establish safety of the receiving controller.  In a third-party
shield-retirement system, bootstrap-only poisoning increased retirement
violations in two of three provenance-complete pairs, with effects varying
across training realizations.  Trusted recomputation closes this channel when
reward semantics are derivable from authenticated transitions.  Otherwise,
deployments must address learning-data integrity, and each
controller receiving authority requires evidence for its handoff contract.
\end{abstract}
