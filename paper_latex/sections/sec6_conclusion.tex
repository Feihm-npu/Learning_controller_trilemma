\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Conditional on bounded write access at an otherwise isolated reward-ingestion
interface, protected adaptation and raw release are different security
contracts.  The distinction is not an artifact of a self-defined benchmark.
On the published shield-retirement lifecycle of Carr et al., bounded
reward-log poisoning confined to the shielded bootstrap phase already makes
the raw policy $1.6$--$3.6\times$ more dangerous at retirement on 3/3 paired
seeds, and the damage persists after fully clean post-retirement learning on
2/3.  A controlled Safe-Control-Gym study then isolates the mechanism: after
a development block locks the target, attacker, and detector, five untouched
seeds yield 27/120 poisoned permanent-release failures versus 2/120 paired
clean.  Resident predictive authority switches before every corresponding
poisoned failure and incurs 0/120 failures; horizons 3, 5, and 10 all separate
the contracts.  An independent audit finds that a one-step resident kernel
still fails on 14/71, so the result depends on recoverability, not residence
alone.

The normalized reward-influence analysis characterizes the batch-local update
authority available through reward corruption.  Conic homogenization makes
positive support exact across normalization singularities and global
gradient-norm clipping, while coordinatewise parameter clipping and
multi-batch attack construction remain outside the theorem.  The broader
cartpole and quadrotor experiments, finite-horizon commit gate, freeze, backup
shield, and official MPSC then expose the coverage, intervention, reward, and
latency costs of different escape contracts.

The integrity boundary is also part of the result.  Trusted recomputation
blocks every evaluated edit, and simple task-aware checks detect over 90\% of
poisoned batches.  Provenance or recomputation removes the upstream attack
precondition; commit admission and resident predictive authority contain its
downstream consequence.  We do not claim a logging-service exploit or a
stealthy attack.

The remaining negative boundaries are part of the result: the target was
development-fixed, the bounded \(\tanh\) perturbation is not a joint gate
optimizer, and even the extended exact-support diagnostic produces no raw
release failure.  The physical attack is established for low-dimensional
Monte-Carlo policy-gradient learners (REINFORCE and a learned-baseline
variant), does not stably transfer to official PPO, and admitted updates do
not beat freeze in the benign-adaptation smoke.  On the third-party lifecycle
the REINFORCE retirement-boundary isolation reproduces on 3/3 seeds and a
ten-seed full-phase REINFORCE battery is partial (4/10), whereas the
third-party PPO full-phase result is positive on 1/3 seeds and neither a
ten-seed full-phase battery (0/10 on the primary at-retirement metric) nor a
ten-seed retirement-boundary isolation battery (1/10) reproduces it; PPO
retirement-boundary isolation is therefore not reproduced at scale, full-scope
poisoning instead persists as unrecovered unsafe released policies (poisoned
final $0.49$--$0.77$ vs.\ clean $\approx0.22$), and the boundary remains
transfer- and training-state-specific.  On a
second learner family (off-policy SAC) the shield-on-only isolation
reproduces on two of three seeds ($0.207 \rightarrow 0.561$ and $0.218
\rightarrow 0.513$), establishing learner variety rather than a single-family
artifact, and a retained-shield control under the same full-scope poisoning
keeps violations at zero on all three seeds ($0/0$) while the paired sudden
runs expose final violations of $0.49$--$0.75$, so resident authority contains
the consequence and removal exposes it.  Formally, an evidence tuple $(s,a,p,i,\tau)$ collected under the training
contract must re-establish each component the deployment contract changes
(Proposition~\ref{prop:transfer}).  Thus
resident predictive evidence is contract-bound; raw release needs independent
future coverage.
