\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

A release test is not runtime authority.  Protected adaptation and permanent
raw release require different security evidence, and the finite check that
authorizes the transition is the evidence that does not survive it.  The same
five-step predictor that accepted every candidate left 2/120 clean and 27/120
poisoned raw-release failures, yet produced none when it kept authority at
runtime; a one-step kernel also kept authority and still failed on 14/71, so
the quantity that mattered was switching lead time rather than residence.  On
Carr et al.'s published shield-retirement workflow, bootstrap-only poisoning
increased raw-policy violations at retirement by factors of $3.6$ and $1.6$ in
two of three provenance-complete paired REINFORCE runs, while protected
execution stayed violation-free, and exploitability remained conditional on
the realized retirement state.  Provenance binding and trusted recomputation
close that channel but not the gap it exploits: evidence stays bound to the
premises that produced it, and raw release must re-establish every one the
transition changes.
