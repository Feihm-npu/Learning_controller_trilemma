\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

A release test is not runtime authority.  Protected adaptation and permanent
raw release require different security evidence, and the finite check that
authorizes the transition is the evidence that does not survive it.  Permanent
raw release failed on 2/120 snapshot--state pairs with a clean learner and
27/120 with a poisoned one, all after the same five-step check accepted them;
resident predictive authority failed on none, a one-step resident kernel
still failed on 14/71, and the horizon sweep bounded the useful lookahead from
both sides.  On Carr et al.'s published shield-retirement workflow, bootstrap-only
poisoning increased raw-policy violations at retirement by factors of $3.6$
and $1.6$ in two of three provenance-complete paired REINFORCE runs, while
protected execution stayed violation-free; exploitability remained conditional
on the realized retirement state.  Provenance binding and trusted
recomputation close that channel but not the gap it exploits: evidence
collected for an assured composition stays bound to its subject, authority,
provenance, information, and horizon, and raw release must re-establish each
obligation it changes.
