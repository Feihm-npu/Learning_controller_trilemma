\section{Third-Party Shield-Retirement Case Study}
\label{sec:third-party}

The controlled contract in Section~\ref{sec:study} is defined by this paper.
To test whether the authority-transition failure is an artifact of a
self-defined benchmark, we repeat the same security question on an
independently published lifecycle that retires runtime authority.
Carr et al.~\cite{carr_partial_shielding_2023} (AAAI 2023) train a
policy-gradient agent for 1000 episodes behind a belief-support shield
computed by Storm on a partially observable gridworld, then either suddenly
or smoothly switch the shield off, and compare the released policy against
retained-shield and no-shield controls.  We use their official public
implementation and change only the learner-visible reward records: the
attacker writes $r'=r+\xi$ with $|\xi|\le\delta$ per record into the update
log, and touches no other plane.  States, observations, shield semantics,
environment dynamics, learner code, hyperparameters, and evaluation are the
upstream originals; every patch is itemized in the artifact.  Training is
5000 episodes for REINFORCE and $10^5$ environment steps for PPO, and
retirement is sudden at episode 1000 (REINFORCE) or env-step $10^5$ (PPO),
following the upstream protocol.

\paragraph{Fidelity gate.}
On the obstacle domain ($N{=}6$), the clean condition reproduces the
qualitative ordering of Carr et al.'s Table~1 for REINFORCE: shield retained
0/0 violations during/after training, smooth 326/675, sudden 3277/3784, and
no-shield 3798/3784.  Shield $<$ smooth $\ll$ sudden $\le$ no-shield is the
ordering the authors report, so the victim workflow is not distorted by our
patch.

\paragraph{Causal isolation at the retirement boundary.}
Under a locked protocol (artifact amendment v1.3), poisoning is confined to
the shielded bootstrap phase: the reward-record mutation is active only while
the shield enforces the action mask and stops exactly at the retirement
instant, so all subsequent learning is clean.  We evaluate the raw policy at
the switch instant before any unshielded learning (1000 episodes) and again
after the full training budget (5000 episodes), on paired seeds.  Table~\ref{tab:carr-isolation}
and Fig.~\ref{fig:carr-isolation} show the result.

On all three seeds the poisoned raw policy is already $1.6$--$3.6\times$
more dangerous at the retirement instant than its clean paired control, while
physical violations during the protected phase remain zero in every run.
The damage is latent in the policy \emph{before} any unshielded learning, and
it is created entirely inside the shielded bootstrap phase.  After 4000
episodes of fully clean post-retirement training the damage persists on two
of three seeds (0.751 and 0.741 vs clean 0.234 and 0.233); one seed
self-heals (0.235).  The mechanism signal is the unmasked
raw-policy/shield disagreement during the protected phase, which stays near
$0.01$--$0.05$ for clean runs but rises to $0.11$--$0.15$ (peak 0.71) under
poisoning while masked violations remain zero: the shield masks unsafe
proposals whose physical consequence appears only after authority removal
(Fig.~\ref{fig:carr-disagreement}).
Across the three paired seeds the per-seed boundary is interpretable: clean
at-retirement fraction and attack headroom are strongly negatively correlated
(Spearman $-1.0$), so the effect concentrates on the seeds whose clean
learning would otherwise transfer safety to the released policy and saturates
on seeds already near the unsafe ceiling.

\paragraph{Learner boundary.}
The same contrast attack ($\delta{=}2$) on the third-party PPO pipeline
(vendored tf-agents PPO with action masking, $10^5$ env steps, sudden
retirement) raises post-removal violations on one of three seeds
(1178 $\rightarrow$ 2439; McNemar exact $p=2.6\times10^{-149}$;
first-violation episode $6\rightarrow0$).  The other two seeds show no
change ($p=1.00$ and $0.96$).  The vulnerability is therefore real and
paired-significant but transfer-sensitive.  It is not a universal learner
property: the effect concentrates on seeds whose clean learning would
otherwise transfer safety to the released policy.  A ten-seed shield-on-only isolation battery under the same locked
protocol reproduces the retirement-boundary effect on one of ten seeds
(seed~101: $0.219\rightarrow0.754$ at retirement; McNemar exact
$p=3.7\times10^{-113}$; first-violation episode $19\rightarrow0$).  The
other nine seeds landed in the unsafe ceiling regime (clean at-retirement
$0.728$--$0.760$) and showed no additional damage: six were significantly
protective and three unchanged.  Fig.~\ref{fig:ppo-isolation} plots the paired at-retirement violation fractions for the full ten-seed battery.  A ten-seed full-phase battery on the same
host under the exact condition of the original claim (contrast
$\delta{=}2$, full training scope) shows the same ceiling: every PPO seed
again landed at the unsafe regime at retirement (clean at-retirement
$0.73$--$0.77$).  No seed was positive on the primary at-retirement metric
(0/10; seven significantly protective, three unchanged), so the full-phase
$1/3$ result does not reproduce at scale either.  The secondary final-release
metric nonetheless shows a persistent consequence that the at-retirement
snapshot misses.  All ten poisoned final policies remain unsafe (violation
fraction $0.49$--$0.77$), while nine of ten clean final policies recover to
$\approx0.22$ under continued learning.  Full-scope contamination blocks
the post-retirement recovery that clean policies exhibit.  PPO
retirement-boundary isolation and full-phase damage are therefore not
reproduced at scale on the primary metric.  The full-phase $1/3$ positive
is training-state-specific rather than a stable on-policy learner property.  A ten-seed
shield-on-only battery on a second learner family from the same vendored
suite (off-policy soft actor-critic, SAC) reproduces the retirement-boundary
effect on three of ten seeds (seed~401: $0.207\rightarrow0.561$; seed~403:
$0.218\rightarrow0.513$; seed~404: $0.479\rightarrow0.954$).  The paired
McNemar exact p-values are $5.6\times10^{-57}$, $7.5\times10^{-41}$, and
$3.5\times10^{-109}$.  None of the seven non-reproducing seeds is a
high-clean-at-retirement ceiling seed ($0.73$--$0.77$).  Three are
significantly protective in the reverse direction ($0.502\rightarrow0.236$,
$p=3.6\times10^{-33}$; $0.497\rightarrow0.247$, $p=8.5\times10^{-30}$;
$0.494\rightarrow0.235$, $p=3.6\times10^{-32}$).  Four show no effect (406:
both at-retirement $0.218$, $p=1.00$; 407: $0.224\rightarrow0.216$, $p=0.71$;
409: $0.220\rightarrow0.232$, $p=0.56$; 410: $0.487\rightarrow0.470$,
$p=0.48$).  Four independently trained extension seeds (407--410) run on a
second host under the identical locked configuration reproduced none of the
positive effect.  The pooled ten-seed verdict is therefore that the SAC
learner-variety evidence weakens (3/10): the off-policy learner-variety claim
is not supported at scale.  The isolation result is therefore not limited to
on-policy Monte-Carlo learners.  The off-policy learner-variety evidence is
partial across seeds and weakens at scale rather than an off-policy
generality.

\paragraph{Attack budget and susceptibility.}
On the full-phase REINFORCE protocol the effect is budget- and
state-dependent (paired seeds within the same locked code version).  On the
transfer-sensitive seed~2, $\delta{=}2$ already saturates the effect:
post-removal violations rise 1127 $\rightarrow$ 3762 ($3.3\times$; McNemar
exact $p\approx0$), and $\delta{=}10$ gives 3766 (no further change).  On
seed~3 the effect requires a larger budget: $\delta{=}2$ leaves violations
unchanged (2482 vs.\ clean 2471; $p=0.84$) while $\delta{=}10$ raises them
to 3700 ($p=8.4\times10^{-138}$).  The seed whose clean released policy is
already in the unsafe regime (seed~1 at 75.7\%) shows no additional damage
at either budget; the bounded additive distortion is absorbed by the value
baseline and return normalization.  Negative controls delimit the attack
shape: the bias variant is protective on the ceiling seed, and the risk
variant is protective on seeds~1 and~3 but raises violations on the
transfer-sensitive seed~2 (1127 $\rightarrow$ 2457, $2.2\times$,
$p=2.0\times10^{-162}$), strictly weaker than the contrast attack on the
same seed.  Figure~\ref{fig:dose-response} shows both boundaries on the
same scale: on the transfer-sensitive seed~2 the effect saturates at
$\delta{=}2$, on seed~3 it requires $\delta{=}10$, and on the ceiling seed
neither budget adds damage while the shape controls are protective.  The
attack is thus not a monotone ``more corruption $\Rightarrow$
more damage'' effect; it is a shape-, budget-, and transfer-state-dependent
steering of the released policy.
  An independent re-run of the full-phase protocol on a Linux host with
  identical dependency versions (15 runs, three seeds $\times$ five
  conditions) completed.  The transfer-sensitive seed on that host
  reproduced the contrast effect at retirement (clean at-retirement 0.232
  $\rightarrow$ poisoned 0.501; post-removal violations 1157 $\rightarrow$
  2451, McNemar exact $p=4.6\times10^{-159}$) with $\delta{=}10$ saturating
  (2450).  The other two seeds landed in the unsafe ceiling regime at
  retirement (clean at-retirement 0.755 and 0.749): contrast $\delta{=}2$
  showed no effect ($p=1.00$) and $\delta{=}10$ was protective, leaving no
  paired headroom for the budget dependence.  The pattern thus reproduces
  across platforms: the effect appears on the transfer-sensitive seed and on
  none of the ceiling seeds, although which seed is transfer-sensitive
  differs (local seed~2 vs.\ server seed~1).  The paired budget dependence
  above is reported from the local locked code version.  A dense budget grid
  ($\delta\in\{0.1,0.25,0.5,1.0\}$) on the server transfer-sensitive seed
  was inconclusive: re-running the identical clean configuration on that host
  landed on a different run-to-run trajectory mode (clean at-retirement
  $0.776$ vs.\ $0.232$ for the same seed and code), so cross-batch budget
  comparisons are not meaningful and the budget dependence is reported only
  within the locked local batch.

\paragraph{Escape condition.}
Under the same full-scope poisoning used in the full-phase battery, a
retained shield (no retirement switch; the final evaluation stays shielded)
keeps physical violations at zero on all three paired seeds through the
final evaluation ($0/0$ on seeds~1--3), while the paired sudden runs expose
final violation fractions of $0.490$ (seed~1), $0.752$ (seed~2), and $0.744$
(seed~3) after removal.  Resident authority therefore contains the downstream
consequence of full-scope contamination; removing it exposes the same
consequence as an unsafe released policy.

\paragraph{Reading.}
The third-party workflow is not a synthetic victim: its shield bootstrap and
retirement schedule are the authors' design, and the release-time raw policy
inherits exactly the update-data provenance assumption the paper
challenges.  The security contribution of this paper is the integrity
dimension on top of that published lifecycle, not the lifecycle itself.
Absolute rates differ across instrumented and non-instrumented code versions
because the unmasked-disagreement probe requires an independent belief
tracker; all clean-vs-poisoned comparisons above are paired within a locked
code version and are unaffected by that version change.
  Absolute rates also vary across platforms.  A ten-seed shield-on-only
  battery on the same Linux host landed seven of the ten independently
  trained REINFORCE seeds in the unsafe regime at retirement (clean
  at-retirement 0.72--0.76), leaving no headroom to measure the attack on
  those seeds.  The three transfer-sensitive seeds all reproduced the
  positive effect (0.240 $\rightarrow$ 0.533, $p=8.3\times10^{-39}$;
  0.234 $\rightarrow$ 0.744, $p=1.9\times10^{-103}$; 0.229 $\rightarrow$
  0.763, $p=6.8\times10^{-114}$); ceiling seeds showed no additional damage
  (two were protective).  The correlation between clean at-retirement
  fraction and attack headroom is also negative on the server battery
  (Spearman $-0.685$, $p=0.029$), consistent with the local value above.
  Across both platforms, both domains, and all three learner families, the
  shield-on-only isolation batteries reproduced the effect on eight of sixteen
  paired seeds with clean at-retirement below 0.5 (8/16: three REINFORCE, one
  PPO, three SAC, and one avoid) and on none of the ceiling seeds (0/17: seven
  REINFORCE, nine PPO, and one SAC).  The eight isolation exceptions are six
  SAC seeds (405 and 408 protective in the reverse direction; 406, 407, 409,
  and 410 with no effect) and the two avoid-domain protective seeds.  The
  clean-below-0.5 rule is therefore a strong tendency rather than a
  deterministic gate, even within a single learner family and across
  environments.  The full-phase batteries at scale on the Linux host reproduce
  the same susceptibility tendency.  Over the locked cross-tabulation of all
  53 paired rows (both scopes, all three learner families, both domains), 12
  of 20 seeds with clean at-retirement below 0.5 reproduced the effect (the
  eight exceptions are the two avoid-domain protective seeds of the
  environment boundary and six SAC non-reproducing seeds), and none of the 33
  ceiling seeds did.  The heterogeneity is platform-, learner-, and
  scope-independent even though the absolute regime differs
  (Fig.~\ref{fig:two-by-two}).

  The susceptibility boundary is not merely a clean-rate threshold: the
  same fifty obstacle paired rows that carry the disagreement probe
  (both scopes, all three learner families) show that the \emph{clean}
  raw-policy/shield disagreement at retirement is the predictor.  Clean
  disagreement is near-monotone in clean at-retirement fraction (Spearman
  $0.976$, $p=1.3\times10^{-33}$): ceiling seeds enter retirement with
  disagreement $\approx0.10$ (clean at-retirement $0.73$--$0.77$) while
  below-0.5 seeds stay $\le0.05$.  Disagreement also
  bounds headroom: across the same rows, the Spearman correlation between
  clean disagreement and attack headroom is $-0.547$
  ($p=3.9\times10^{-5}$), and every positive row has clean disagreement
  $\le0.047$ while non-positive rows reach $0.111$.  This reframes the
  PPO boundary.  19 of 20 PPO rows land in the high-disagreement regime
  ($\ge0.09$) with clean at-retirement $0.73$--$0.77$, leaving no headroom
  to measure the attack, and the single low-disagreement PPO row (isolation
  seed~101, disagreement $0.013$) is exactly the one that reproduced.  PPO's
  at-scale negatives are therefore a headroom artifact of the
  training-state regime it samples rather than evidence against
  transfer-sensitivity.  REINFORCE is mixed (13/20 high-disagreement, all
  seven low-disagreement rows reproducing).  SAC is the cleanest check:
  all ten SAC rows are low-disagreement ($0.013$--$0.048$), so SAC never
  leaves the headroom-available regime, and the pooled verdict is genuinely
  partial across seeds (3/10: seeds 401/403/404 positive, 402/405/408
  protective, 406/407/409/410 no effect).  The divergence signal is consistent
  across all three learner families on the obstacle domain.  The signal is
  obstacle-scoped: the avoid-domain seeds do not follow it (clean
  disagreement $0.476$ and $0.157$ on protective seeds below the $0.5$
  boundary).  The mechanism claim is therefore restricted to this lifecycle.

\paragraph{Environment boundary.}
The lifecycle pattern is not obstacle-specific: on a second gridworld of
the same third-party suite (surveillance, $N{=}6$, $R{=}3$), a clean
reproduction again satisfies the authors' ordering, shield retained $0/0$,
smooth $0.129$, sudden $0.246$, and no-shield $0.252$ (retained $<$
smooth $\ll$ sudden $\le$ no-shield).  The fixed attack shape, however,
does not transfer as a general property: shield-on contrast poisoning at
$\delta{=}2$ on three independent avoid seeds raised post-removal
violations on only one seed ($0.000 \rightarrow 0.272$, McNemar exact
$p=2.6\times10^{-82}$) and was significantly protective on the other two
($0.267 \rightarrow 0.000$, $p=8.4\times10^{-81}$; $0.135 \rightarrow
0.038$, $p=1.3\times10^{-15}$).  The clean lifecycle pattern therefore
generalizes across environments, while the corruption effect is
environment- and training-state-sensitive rather than a universal property
of the workflow.
