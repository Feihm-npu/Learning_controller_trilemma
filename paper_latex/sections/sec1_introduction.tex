\section{Introduction}

Runtime assurance can keep an adaptive controller physically safe without
establishing that its raw policy is safe.  A resident decision module may
replace unsafe policy proposals with actions from a trusted baseline while
corrupted update data continue to influence learning.  The assured
composition remains protected, but the raw policy can accumulate latent
damage.  A security boundary appears when the decision module is retired and
the raw policy inherits action authority.

Existing assurance and learning-security mechanisms address complementary
parts of this lifecycle.  Simplex retains a decision module and an assured
baseline controller~\cite{seto_simplex_1998}.  Neural and barrier-based Simplex
extend this model with online retraining and stronger switching conditions
\cite{phan_neural_simplex_2020,damare_bb_simplex_2025}.  Policy repair and safe
projection constrain learned policies before deployment
\cite{zhou_runtime_policy_repair_2020,lu_repair_preservation_2024,
chow_safe_policy_2021}.  Reward-poisoning work, by contrast, studies how
adversarial learning signals redirect policy updates.  The unresolved
composition is an authority transition in which protected adaptation uses an
adversarial update log, followed by deployment of the raw policy without the
mechanism that governed data collection.

We examine this composition under an explicit split-trust precondition.
Operational technology (OT) guidance separates runtime-control assets from
data-management services and historians that exchange information across
zones~\cite{nist_ot_security_2023,mitre_data_historian}.  Our threat model
begins after compromise of a reward-ingestion principal in this learning-data
plane.  The attacker can modify bounded scalar reward records consumed by a
policy-gradient updater.  Authenticated runtime states, actions, learner code,
parameters, optimizer state, certificates, and deployment states remain
outside its write authority.  Trusted recomputation from authenticated
transitions blocks the evaluated channel when reward semantics permit it.

The attack targets the evidence used to authorize raw release.  Bounded reward
edits steer the raw policy toward a target that passes a finite release check
but can fail later after runtime authority is removed.  We call the resulting
update \emph{certificate-evasive} with respect to that finite release contract.
The term does not imply evasion of reward-integrity checks or sound invariant
certificates.

For a fixed policy-gradient batch, bounded reward edits induce a structured
set of possible parameter updates.  Advantage standardization makes this set
non-affine, so we derive its normalized reward-influence geometry.  Conic
homogenization then yields exact positive support against linear release-gate
halfspaces under global gradient-norm clipping.  This analysis characterizes
batch-local influence; the evaluated multi-batch attack remains a fixed,
bounded target-shaping rule.

\subsection{Evidence Stack and Contributions}

The paper combines an independently designed lifecycle with a controlled
mechanism study.  Carr et al.'s published workflow trains a policy behind a
runtime shield and later retires the shield's authority
\cite{carr_partial_shielding_2023}.  We add bounded reward-record poisoning
only during the protected bootstrap.  In two of three provenance-complete
paired REINFORCE training runs, poisoning increased raw-policy violations at
retirement by factors of 3.6 and 1.6.  A freshly regenerated third pair was
unchanged despite low clean disagreement.  The controlled Safe-Control-Gym
study isolated the same transition and compared one-time raw release with
resident predictive authority, commit admission, and permanent filtering.
Together, the studies support an architectural vulnerability with conditional
exploitability.

Our contributions are:

\begin{itemize}
    \item We formalize an authority-transition vulnerability through an
    evidence tuple covering subject, authority, provenance, information, and
    horizon.  Deployment must re-establish each component changed by the
    transition.
    \item We instantiate this vulnerability in Carr et al.'s third-party
    shield-retirement lifecycle.  The paired positive and non-positive
    training realizations show both the exposure and the conditional nature of
    the fixed attack.
    \item We derive exact batch-local reward influence on linear release-gate
    margins under standardized advantages and global gradient-norm clipping.
    Controlled experiments then separate permanent raw release from resident
    predictive authority and one-step filtering.
    \item We characterize the empirical boundary of the attack across systems,
    learner implementations, integrity controls, and deployment contracts.
    High retirement-state disagreement excludes every observed positive
    outcome in the locked obstacle corpus, while low disagreement defines an
    opportunity region without guaranteeing success.
\end{itemize}
