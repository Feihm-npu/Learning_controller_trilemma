\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\textbf{Runtime assurance and safe controller updates.}
Simplex architectures retain a decision module and a trusted baseline during
online upgrades~\cite{seto_simplex_1998}.  Neural and barrier-based Simplex
extend this model through online retraining, reverse switching, and switching
conditions under bounded model error
\cite{phan_neural_simplex_2020,damare_bb_simplex_2025}.  Model-predictive
safety certification (MPSC) and runtime shields similarly preserve
recoverability or prevent unsafe actions
\cite{wabersich_linear_mpsc_2018,didier_adaptive_mpsc_2021,
alshiekh_shielding_2018}.  These mechanisms certify a resident composition.
Our setting instead considers a transition in which the raw policy later
receives action authority.

Policy repair, safe projection, joint controller/certificate repair, and
robust conformal transfer establish evidence for an updated policy before use
\cite{zhou_runtime_policy_repair_2020,lu_repair_preservation_2024,
chow_safe_policy_2021,yu_certificate_repair_2025,
mirzaeedodangeh_safe_updates_2026}.  Shielded transfer likewise collects
independent generalization evidence before removing runtime authority
\cite{hsu_sim_lab_real_2023}.  These approaches re-establish evidence for the
deployment object.  The present work studies the complementary case in which
protected adaptation is followed by raw release under adversarial update
provenance.

\textbf{Shield retirement and attacked state semantics.}
Carr et al.~\cite{carr_partial_shielding_2023} study a partially observable
bootstrap-to-retirement lifecycle and compare sudden with smooth shield
removal.  Their learning signals are trusted, and their study evaluates the
behavioral effect of shield removal.  We use this published lifecycle as a
third-party case study and add bounded reward-record poisoning during the
protected bootstrap.  The resulting experiment concerns evidence reuse across
the same authority transition, not the soundness of Carr et al.'s original
claims.

Secure estimation characterizes when sensor attacks can be detected or
corrected~\cite{pasqualetti_attack_2013,fawzi_secure_2014}.  Severe-attack CBF
filtering constructs plausible-state sets and safe-action kernels when the
state cannot be reconstructed uniquely~\cite{tan_safety_2024}.  Our formal
model consumes such a plausible-state set and asks whether an adversarially
updated action map remains inside its certified kernel.  Related lines learn
barriers, certify reinforcement-learning robustness, or solve adversarial
reach--avoid problems under trusted learning signals
\cite{brunke_barrier_learning_2022,capone_bandit_safe_control_2025,
wu_certifying_2022,pham_certified_2024,hsu_isaacs_2023,
nguyen_gameplay_2024}.

\textbf{Policy and reward poisoning.}
Policy poisoning has been studied in batch reinforcement learning and control
\cite{ma_policy_poisoning_2019}.  Online attacks can perturb individual rewards
under an $\ell_\infty$ budget and adapt to the learning process
\cite{zhang_reward_poisoning_2020}.  Extensions cover policy-gradient learners
without known dynamics and black-box deep reinforcement learning
\cite{sun_online_rl_poisoning_2021,xu_deep_reward_poisoning_2025}.  Targeted
reward or environment poisoning can teach a chosen policy, and corrupted
reward channels are a recognized reinforcement-learning hazard
\cite{rakhsha_policy_teaching_2020,everitt_corrupted_reward_2017}.

BADControl poisons operational data in PID and LQR loops to implant a
vulnerability activated by a physical trigger~\cite{burbano_badcontrol_2026}.
Our contribution concerns a different composition: bounded reward-record
poisoning affects a policy trained behind runtime assurance, and failure
appears after authority retirement without an activation input.  Online
reward-poisoning defenses model adaptive attacker--defender interaction
\cite{nika_reward_defense_2023}.  We instead separate authenticated
provenance, trusted recomputation, semantics-dependent detection, commit
admission, and downstream runtime containment.

\textbf{Conic reward-influence analysis.}
Fractional programs can be homogenized into convex forms under suitable
numerator and denominator structure~\cite{schaible_fractional_duality_1976}.
Disciplined quasiconvex programming provides the associated fixed-level
feasibility and bisection pattern~\cite{agrawal_dqcp_2020}.  Projection and
feasibility over homogenization and second-order cones are classical
\cite{bauschke_homogenization_2023,belloni_soc_feasibility_2008}.  We apply
these tools to the normalized image of a centered reward-to-go zonotope.
Global gradient-norm clipping introduces a second norm branch, while
unit-positive-numerator normalization removes the singular feasible point.

\section{Discussion}

The evidence supports an architectural vulnerability with conditional
exploitability.  Protected adaptation certifies the executed composition while
resident authority remains active.  Permanent raw release changes the subject,
authority, provenance, information, and horizon of that evidence.  The
third-party case study locates this transition in a published lifecycle, and
the controlled study isolates the effect of adversarial reward provenance.
Neither result requires the shield or filter itself to be unsound.

The attack has a structured opportunity region, but entry into that region
does not determine success.  Across the locked obstacle corpus, every positive
outcome occurred at low retirement-state disagreement, and no
high-disagreement run was positive.  This pattern makes high disagreement a
strong empirical exclusion condition.  Low disagreement instead indicates
available headroom.  The fresh REINFORCE pair, seven low-band corpus runs, and
the SAC battery show that headroom is insufficient for the fixed attack.
Attackability therefore follows the realized retirement state more closely
than the configured seed identifier, while residual learner and trajectory
dependence remains within the opportunity region.

This distinction also bounds the operational meaning of the marker.
Retirement-state disagreement is observed after the protected bootstrap and
therefore characterizes a realized deployment state.  It is not an attacker-
side trigger available before poisoning begins.  Such a trigger would require
evidence that earlier disagreement trajectories predict the eventual
opportunity region.  The reported AUC is consequently a descriptive measure
over the locked corpus rather than an externally validated classifier.

The defense results separate integrity from authority.  Provenance binding
and trusted reward recomputation remove the evaluated upstream channel.
Task-aware checks detect the effective fixed attack under additional semantic
assumptions.  Commit admission limits which raw snapshot receives authority,
whereas resident predictive authority and permanent filtering contain unsafe
policy proposals at runtime.  The one-step resident kernel shows that
residence alone is insufficient; the decision rule must preserve
recoverability over the relevant horizon.  These contracts incur different
coverage, intervention, latency, and reward costs.

The formal results have bounded verification scope.  The current- and
future-history gates state certificate obligations, and the linear parameter
gate is exact on its finite abstraction.  The continuous lift requires a
justified joint reachable-set cover, regularity bounds, model-error bounds,
and a robust margin.  The empirical coverage audit probes these premises but
does not establish continuous-state invariance.  The exact reward-influence
result covers a fixed standardized-advantage batch with global gradient
clipping.  Coordinatewise parameter clipping and multi-batch attack
construction remain outside that result.

The empirical generality is also bounded.  The end-to-end physical effect is
established for low-dimensional Monte Carlo policy-gradient learners.  The
learned-baseline variant shares the same return and attack channel rather than
forming an independent learner family.  The official Safe-Control-Gym PPO
audit showed checkpoint-level redirection without stable physical failure.
On the third-party lifecycle, PPO and SAC contained individual positive
training realizations but did not provide reliable success across paired runs.
The retirement-state marker is obstacle-scoped; the second environment also
contained protective outcomes.

Finally, the threat model begins after bounded reward-ingestion access.  It
does not estimate the prevalence of that access across products or model
service exploitation.  The effective fixed attack is detectable by the tested
task-aware checks, and the sign-respecting variants did not retain deployment
harm.  The trusted-anchor audit models bounded error only for altitude and
pitch and reports calls rather than hardware-specific latency, energy,
authentication, or monetary cost.  These boundaries limit claims about
stealth, cross-architecture prevalence, and deployment overhead without
changing the authority-transition result.
